# Enterprise Ownership Model

> "Ownership is not the authority to control—it is the responsibility to sustain, improve, and protect."

---

# Purpose

This document defines the official ownership model for the Avonix AI Enterprise Documentation Repository.

It establishes ownership principles, accountability boundaries, stewardship responsibilities, delegation rules, ownership transitions, succession planning, and governance controls that ensure every repository artifact has clear and continuous ownership throughout its lifecycle.

The Ownership Model eliminates ambiguity by ensuring that every document, process, policy, and decision has a defined accountable owner.

---

# Vision

Create an ownership model that is:

- Accountable
- Transparent
- Sustainable
- Traceable
- Scalable
- Governed
- Collaborative
- Enterprise-Ready

Ownership should enable responsible stewardship rather than exclusive control.

---

# Objectives

The Ownership Model aims to:

- Define ownership responsibilities
- Clarify accountability
- Prevent ownership gaps
- Enable delegated stewardship
- Support governance continuity
- Improve lifecycle management
- Strengthen audit readiness
- Preserve institutional knowledge

---

# Ownership Principles

Ownership throughout the repository should follow these principles.

## Accountability

Every governed artifact must have one clearly accountable owner.

---

## Stewardship

Owners are responsible for maintaining long-term quality and relevance.

---

## Transparency

Ownership assignments should be visible and documented.

---

## Continuity

Ownership should survive personnel, organizational, or structural changes.

---

## Delegation

Execution responsibilities may be delegated, but accountability remains with the assigned owner.

---

## Traceability

Ownership history should be preserved for governance and audit purposes.

---

# Ownership Scope

The Ownership Model applies to:

- Repository
- Documentation Layers
- Standards
- Policies
- Templates
- Decisions
- Architecture Documents
- Governance Artifacts
- Quality Frameworks
- Operational Procedures

Every governed artifact must fall within the ownership framework.

# Ownership Types

## Strategic Owner

Responsible for:

- Long-term direction
- Repository vision
- Governance evolution
- Strategic approvals

Typical role:

Enterprise Architecture Council

---

## Governance Owner

Responsible for:

- Governance policies
- Compliance
- Oversight
- Policy consistency

Typical role:

Governance Council

---

## Operational Owner

Responsible for:

- Daily repository management
- Publication coordination
- Repository maintenance
- Operational improvements

Typical role:

Repository Maintainer

---

## Document Owner

Responsible for:

- Content quality
- Metadata
- Reviews
- Lifecycle management
- Version updates

Every governed document should have exactly one accountable Document Owner.

---

## Contributor

Responsible for:

- Proposed improvements
- Issue reporting
- Content suggestions
- Collaborative contributions

Contributors improve documentation but do not assume ownership by default.

# Ownership Matrix

|----------|-------------------|------------------|
| Repository Vision | Enterprise Architecture Council | Governance Council |

| Repository Structure | Repository Maintainer | Document Owners |
| Documentation | Document Owner | Reviewer, Contributors |
| Templates | Governance Council | Technical Writers |

| Decision Records | Decision Authority | Repository Maintainer |

This matrix establishes primary accountability while recognizing collaborative support.

---

# Ownership Responsibilities

Every accountable owner should:

- Maintain accuracy
- Coordinate reviews
- Approve updates within authority
- Ensure lifecycle compliance
- Resolve assigned issues
- Respond to audit findings
- Maintain metadata
- Preserve traceability

Ownership includes both maintenance and governance responsibilities.

---

# Delegation Model

Delegation may occur for:

- Editorial updates
- Technical reviews
- Metadata maintenance
- Administrative tasks
- Publication coordination

Delegated work does not transfer accountability.

---

# Ownership Transition

Ownership should be transferred when:

- Responsibilities change
- Organizational structures evolve
- Repository restructuring occurs
- Stewardship responsibilities are reassigned

Transitions should include:

- Formal approval
- Updated ownership records
- Knowledge transfer
- Open issue review
- Related document updates

Transitions should be documented to preserve continuity.

---

# Succession Planning

Each critical ownership role should identify:

- Successor role
- Transition trigger
- Knowledge transfer expectations
- Repository responsibilities
- Outstanding governance obligations

Succession planning reduces operational risk.

---

# Ownership Verification

Ownership should be verified during:

- Governance reviews
- Lifecycle reviews
- Audit activities
- Release validation
- Compliance assessments

Verification ensures accountability remains current.

---

# Ownership Exceptions

Ownership exceptions should:

- Be documented
- Include justification
- Receive governance approval
- Define temporary accountability
- Include an expiration or review date

Long-term unassigned ownership is not permitted.

---

# Ownership Risks

Common ownership risks include:

- Missing owners
- Multiple accountable owners
- Outdated ownership records
- Knowledge loss
- Unclear authority
- Delayed ownership transitions
- Unmanaged delegated responsibilities

These risks should be monitored through governance activities.

---

# Ownership Metrics

Monitor ownership effectiveness through:

| Metric | Description |
|--------|-------------|

Metrics should be used to strengthen governance and continuity.
