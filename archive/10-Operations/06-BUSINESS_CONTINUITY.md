# Business Continuity Standard

> "Resilient organizations continue delivering value even when conditions are far from normal."

---

# Purpose

This document defines the canonical Business Continuity Standard for Avonix AI.

It establishes the governance, planning principles, operational responsibilities, and resilience practices required to maintain critical business operations during disruptive events while protecting customers, employees, partners, and organizational objectives.

---

# Philosophy

Business continuity should be:

- Business-driven
- Risk-aware
- Customer-focused
- Operationally resilient
- Continuously tested
- Measurable
- Continuously improving

Business continuity extends beyond technology and encompasses people, processes, facilities, vendors, and governance.

---

# Objectives

This standard should ensure:

- Continuity of critical business services
- Protection of customer commitments
- Minimized operational disruption
- Coordinated crisis response
- Predictable recovery planning
- Organizational resilience

---

# Scope

Applies to:

- Business operations
- Customer-facing services
- Engineering teams
- Operations teams
- AI services
- Infrastructure
- Vendors and partners
- Corporate functions

---

# Business Continuity Principles

Every continuity strategy should emphasize:

- Preparation before disruption
- Clear decision-making
- Defined ownership
- Documented recovery priorities
- Cross-functional coordination
- Continuous validation
- Lessons learned

---

# Business Impact Analysis (BIA)

Each critical business capability should identify:

- Business function
- Criticality
- Maximum acceptable downtime
- Recovery priority
- Operational dependencies
- Customer impact
- Financial impact
- Regulatory impact

The BIA should guide continuity planning and investment decisions.

---

# Critical Business Services

Critical services should be categorized according to:

- Customer impact
- Revenue impact
- Legal obligations
- Operational importance
- Brand reputation
- Strategic value

Criticality classifications should remain consistent across the organization.

---

# Dependency Mapping

Continuity planning should document dependencies across:

- People
- Applications
- Infrastructure
- Data
- AI services
- Third-party vendors
- Network connectivity
- Security services

Dependencies should be regularly reviewed and updated.

---

# Continuity Strategies

Business continuity strategies should address:

- Workforce continuity
- Technology continuity
- Data continuity
- Vendor continuity
- Facility continuity
- Customer communication continuity

Strategies should prioritize restoration of the highest-value business capabilities.

---

# Crisis Management

The crisis management framework should define:

- Crisis declaration criteria
- Crisis management team
- Decision authority
- Escalation process
- Communication responsibilities
- Situation reporting
- Recovery coordination

Major decisions should be documented throughout the event.

---

# Roles & Responsibilities

Business continuity responsibilities may include:

- Executive Sponsor
- Business Continuity Manager
- Incident Commander
- Operations Lead
- Engineering Lead
- Security Lead
- Communications Lead
- Business Unit Representatives

Responsibilities should be clearly assigned before any disruption occurs.

---

# Communication Continuity

Continuity communications should support:

- Internal teams
- Customers
- Partners
- Vendors
- Regulators
- Executive leadership

Communications should remain timely, accurate, and consistent.

---

# Third-Party Resilience

Vendor continuity planning should evaluate:

- Service dependencies
- Provider resilience
- Alternative providers
- Contractual obligations
- Escalation procedures
- Recovery commitments

Critical vendors should participate in continuity planning where appropriate.

---

# Workforce Continuity

Plans should address:

- Remote operations
- Alternate staffing
- Knowledge transfer
- Leadership succession
- Workforce availability
- Critical role coverage

Operational knowledge should not depend on a single individual.

---

# Disaster Recovery Alignment

Business continuity should align with:

- Disaster Recovery
- Backup & Recovery
- Incident Response
- SRE
- Monitoring & Observability

Technology recovery should support business recovery priorities.

---

# Continuity Testing

Continuity plans should be validated through:

- Tabletop exercises
- Simulation exercises
- Recovery drills
- Communication tests
- Cross-functional rehearsals

Testing should occur on a defined schedule and after significant organizational or architectural changes.

---

# Documentation

Every continuity plan should document:

- Business objectives
- Critical services
- Recovery priorities
- Recovery procedures
- Roles and responsibilities
- Communication plans
- External dependencies
- Review history

Documentation should remain synchronized with operational reality.

---

# Continuous Improvement

Business continuity should improve through:

- Exercise outcomes
- Incident reviews
- Risk assessments
- Process refinement
- Automation opportunities
- Organizational learning

Improvement actions should be tracked to completion.

---

# Governance

Business continuity governance requires:

- Executive review
- Operations review
- Engineering review
- Security review
- Annual program assessment

Governance should ensure organization-wide consistency and accountability.

---

# Success Metrics

Business continuity effectiveness may be evaluated through:

- Business Recovery Time Objective (RTO) achievement
- Recovery Point Objective (RPO) achievement
- Exercise completion rate
- Critical service availability
- Recovery readiness score
- Vendor resilience performance
- Customer-impact duration
