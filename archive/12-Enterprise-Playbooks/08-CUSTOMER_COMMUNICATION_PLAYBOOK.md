# Enterprise Customer Communication Playbook

> "Clear, timely, and trustworthy communication preserves customer confidence before, during, and after every business event."

---

# Purpose

This playbook defines the canonical Enterprise Customer Communication framework for Avonix AI.

It establishes a governance-aligned process for planning, approving, delivering, monitoring, and reviewing customer communications across operational, technical, security, and business events.

---

# Philosophy

Customer communication should be:

- Transparent
- Accurate
- Timely
- Consistent
- Empathetic
- Accessible
- Actionable

Communication should reduce uncertainty while strengthening customer trust.

---

# Objectives

This playbook ensures:

- Consistent messaging
- Coordinated communication
- Customer confidence
- Regulatory alignment
- Executive visibility
- Brand consistency
- Continuous improvement

---

# Scope

Applies to:

- Product launches
- Scheduled maintenance
- Service incidents
- Security events
- Disaster recovery
- AI capability releases
- Planned platform migrations
- Policy changes
- Business announcements

---

# Communication Principles

Every communication should prioritize:

- Accuracy
- Clarity
- Relevance
- Accessibility
- Transparency
- Consistency
- Customer value

---

# Communication Lifecycle

Every customer communication should progress through:

```text
Prepare
      ↓
Review
      ↓
Approve
      ↓
Notify
      ↓
Monitor
      ↓
Update
      ↓
Resolution
      ↓
Follow-up
      ↓
Retrospective
      ↓
Continuous Improvement
```

---

# Phase 1 — Prepare

Define:

- Business context
- Communication objective
- Target audience
- Communication urgency
- Delivery channels
- Expected outcomes

Preparation should align messaging with organizational goals.

---

# Phase 2 — Review

Review communication for:

- Accuracy
- Technical correctness
- Legal considerations
- Security implications
- Compliance requirements
- Brand alignment

Cross-functional review should occur before publication.

---

# Phase 3 — Approval

Approval authority should reflect communication impact.

Approvers may include:

- Product Leadership
- Customer Experience
- Security Leadership
- Legal
- Compliance
- Executive Sponsor

---

# Audience Segmentation

Communications may target:

- Individual customers
- Enterprise customers
- Partners
- Vendors
- Internal teams
- Executive leadership
- Regulators (where applicable)
- Media (where applicable)

Each audience should receive information appropriate to its needs.

---

# Communication Categories

Examples include:

- Product release announcements
- Maintenance notifications
- Incident updates
- Security advisories
- Service restoration notices
- Policy updates
- Feature deprecation notices
- AI capability announcements

Each category should follow documented approval and delivery standards.

---

# Communication Channels

Approved channels may include:

- Email
- In-app notifications
- Status page
- Customer portal
- SMS
- Push notifications
- Support portal
- Social media
- Knowledge base

Channel selection should reflect urgency, audience, and business impact.

---

# Message Standards

Every communication should define:

- What happened
- Who is affected
- Business impact
- Customer impact
- Actions customers should take
- Current status
- Expected next update
- Support contact information

Messages should avoid unnecessary technical jargon.

---

# Accessibility

Communications should support:

- Plain language
- Readable formatting
- Mobile accessibility
- Multilingual delivery (where applicable)
- Accessibility standards compliance

Accessibility should ensure equitable customer access to information.

---

# Incident Communication

During incidents communicate:

- Incident confirmation
- Current impact
- Mitigation progress
- Estimated next update
- Resolution confirmation

Updates should remain factual and avoid speculation.

---

# Maintenance Communication

Maintenance notices should include:

- Maintenance window
- Expected impact
- Services affected
- Customer actions (if any)
- Completion confirmation

---

# Security Communication

Security-related communications should include:

- Nature of the issue
- Customer impact
- Recommended actions
- Available support
- Follow-up updates

Sensitive investigative details should not be disclosed unless appropriate.

---

# AI Communication

For AI releases communicate:

- New capabilities
- Known limitations
- Responsible AI expectations
- User guidance
- Feedback mechanisms

Customers should understand both benefits and limitations.

---

# Communication Monitoring

Monitor:

- Delivery success
- Open rates
- Customer engagement
- Support volume
- Customer sentiment
- Escalations
- Feedback trends

Monitoring should guide follow-up communication.

---

# Documentation

Maintain:

- Communication plan
- Approval records
- Published messages
- Distribution logs
- Customer feedback
- Communication metrics
- Lessons learned

Documentation should remain complete and audit-ready.

---

# Success Metrics

Communication effectiveness may be measured through:

- Delivery success rate
- Customer engagement
- Support ticket reduction
- Communication timeliness
- Customer satisfaction
- Message clarity
- Trust and sentiment trends

---

# Continuous Improvement

Improve communication through:

- Customer feedback
- Incident retrospectives
- Communication audits
- Accessibility reviews
- Brand consistency assessments
- Governance recommendations

Every communication should improve future customer engagement.

---

# Governance

Customer communication governance requires:

- Customer Experience Council review
- Product approval
- Security review (where applicable)
- Legal and Compliance review (where applicable)
- Executive approval for enterprise-impacting communications

No high-impact customer communication should be published without appropriate governance.
