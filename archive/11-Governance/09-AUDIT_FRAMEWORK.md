# Enterprise Audit Framework Standard

> "Audit provides independent assurance that governance is operating as intended and that organizational commitments are supported by objective evidence."

---

# Purpose

This document defines the canonical Enterprise Audit Framework Standard for Avonix AI.

It establishes the governance model, audit lifecycle, planning methodology, evidence management practices, reporting expectations, and continuous improvement framework required to independently evaluate the effectiveness of governance, controls, operational processes, and compliance activities across the enterprise.

---

# Philosophy

Audit should be:

- Independent
- Objective
- Risk-based
- Evidence-driven
- Transparent
- Constructive
- Continuously improving

Audits should strengthen governance and operational excellence rather than function solely as compliance exercises.

---

# Objectives

This standard should ensure:

- Independent organizational assurance
- Consistent audit execution
- Reliable evidence collection
- Effective corrective action
- Executive visibility
- Regulatory readiness
- Continuous governance improvement

---

# Scope

Applies to:

- Governance processes
- Enterprise policies
- Security controls
- AI governance
- Data governance
- Technology governance
- Operational processes
- Third-party oversight
- Regulatory obligations
- Internal standards

---

# Audit Principles

Every audit should emphasize:

- Independence
- Integrity
- Objectivity
- Professional judgment
- Evidence-based conclusions
- Confidentiality
- Continuous improvement

Audit activities should remain free from conflicts of interest.

---

# Audit Governance

Audit governance should establish:

- Enterprise Audit Council
- Internal Audit function
- External Audit coordination
- Executive oversight
- Audit approval authority
- Audit reporting responsibilities

Governance should preserve auditor independence while ensuring organizational accountability.

---

# Audit Types

The framework should support:

- Internal audits
- External audits
- Regulatory audits
- Compliance audits
- Security audits
- Technology audits
- AI governance audits
- Operational audits
- Supplier audits

Each audit type should follow a consistent governance model while addressing domain-specific requirements.

---

# Risk-Based Audit Planning

Audit planning should consider:

- Business criticality
- Risk assessments
- Regulatory obligations
- Incident history
- Organizational change
- Previous audit findings
- Executive priorities

Higher-risk areas should receive greater audit attention.

---

# Audit Lifecycle

Every audit should progress through:

```text
Plan
      ↓
Prepare
      ↓
Execute
      ↓
Evaluate Evidence
      ↓
Report Findings
      ↓
Corrective Actions
      ↓
Verification
      ↓
Closure
```

Each stage should include documented responsibilities, deliverables, and approval criteria.

---

# Audit Scope

Every audit should define:

- Objectives
- Scope boundaries
- Applicable standards
- Evaluation criteria
- Stakeholders
- Required evidence
- Expected deliverables

The audit scope should be approved before execution.

---

# Evidence Management

Audit evidence may include:

- Policies
- Standards
- Operational procedures
- System configurations
- Logs
- Metrics
- Approval records
- Training records
- Interview summaries
- Observation records

Evidence should be authentic, complete, traceable, and retained according to organizational policies.

---

# Findings Classification

Audit findings should be categorized by:

- Critical
- High
- Medium
- Low
- Observation
- Opportunity for Improvement

Classification should reflect organizational impact and urgency.

---

# Corrective Action Governance

Every finding should define:

- Root cause
- Corrective action
- Preventive action
- Responsible owner
- Target completion date
- Verification criteria

Corrective actions should remain open until independently verified.

---

# Follow-Up Verification

Verification activities should confirm:

- Corrective actions completed
- Controls operating effectively
- Risks appropriately addressed
- Documentation updated
- Evidence retained

Verification should precede formal audit closure.

---

# Audit Reporting

Audit reports should summarize:

- Audit objectives
- Scope
- Methodology
- Key findings
- Risk implications
- Corrective actions
- Executive summary
- Overall assurance opinion

Reports should be accurate, balanced, and understandable by both technical and business stakeholders.

---

# Audit Record Retention

Audit records should maintain:

- Audit plans
- Evidence
- Findings
- Reports
- Corrective action history
- Verification records
- Closure approvals

Retention periods should align with regulatory and organizational requirements.

---

# Documentation

The audit framework should maintain:

- Annual audit plan
- Audit register
- Evidence repository
- Finding register
- Corrective action tracker
- Audit metrics
- Lessons learned

Documentation should remain complete, secure, and audit-ready.

---

# Continuous Improvement

Audit effectiveness should improve through:

- Trend analysis
- Root cause reviews
- Methodology refinement
- Stakeholder feedback
- Regulatory updates
- Governance assessments

Continuous improvement should enhance organizational assurance over time.

---

# Governance

Audit governance requires:

- Enterprise Audit Council review
- Compliance review
- Risk review
- Executive oversight
- Independent reporting authority

Audit governance should preserve independence while supporting enterprise decision-making.

---

# Success Metrics

Audit framework effectiveness may be evaluated through:

- Audit plan completion rate
- Finding closure rate
- Corrective action verification rate
- Repeat finding reduction
- Audit cycle time
- Evidence completeness
- Organizational assurance maturity
