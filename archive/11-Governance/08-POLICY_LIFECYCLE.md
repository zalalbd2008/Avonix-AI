# Enterprise Policy Lifecycle Standard

> "Policies create organizational direction. Governance ensures they remain accurate, relevant, enforceable, and continuously improved."

---

# Purpose

This document defines the canonical Enterprise Policy Lifecycle Standard for Avonix AI.

It establishes the governance framework, lifecycle stages, ownership model, approval process, version management, communication expectations, and continuous review practices required to manage enterprise policies throughout their entire lifecycle.

---

# Philosophy

Policy governance should be:

- Consistent
- Transparent
- Traceable
- Business-aligned
- Auditable
- Maintainable
- Continuously evolving

Policies should remain living governance assets rather than static documentation.

---

# Objectives

This standard should ensure:

- Consistent policy creation
- Clear ownership
- Controlled approvals
- Organization-wide communication
- Effective implementation
- Continuous review
- Orderly retirement of obsolete policies

---

# Scope

Applies to:

- Enterprise policies
- Governance standards
- Organizational directives
- Security policies
- AI policies
- Data policies
- Operational policies
- Compliance policies
- Technology policies
- Supporting governance documentation

---

# Policy Governance Principles

Every policy should emphasize:

- Business value
- Clarity
- Enforceability
- Accountability
- Consistency
- Measurability
- Continuous improvement

Policies should guide decision-making without creating unnecessary administrative burden.

---

# Policy Lifecycle

Every policy should progress through:

```text
Identify
      ↓
Draft
      ↓
Review
      ↓
Approve
      ↓
Publish
      ↓
Implement
      ↓
Monitor
      ↓
Review
      ↓
Revise or Retire
```

Each stage should include documented ownership, review criteria, and approval requirements.

---

# Policy Classification

Policies may be classified according to:

- Enterprise-wide
- Domain-specific
- Operational
- Technical
- Regulatory
- Temporary
- Emergency

Classification should determine governance rigor and review frequency.

---

# Policy Ownership

Each policy should identify:

- Executive Sponsor
- Policy Owner
- Document Author
- Domain Reviewer
- Compliance Owner
- Version Custodian

Ownership should remain documented for the duration of the policy lifecycle.

---

# Policy Development

Policy development should include:

- Business justification
- Scope definition
- Stakeholder consultation
- Risk assessment
- Dependency identification
- Impact analysis

Development should ensure policies are practical and aligned with organizational objectives.

---

# Review & Approval

Policy reviews should evaluate:

- Strategic alignment
- Legal implications
- Security considerations
- Operational feasibility
- Technical consistency
- Compliance impact

Approval authority should correspond to the policy's organizational impact.

---

# Publication & Communication

Approved policies should include:

- Publication date
- Effective date
- Target audience
- Communication plan
- Acknowledgment requirements
- Supporting guidance

Publication should ensure stakeholders have timely access to current policies.

---

# Implementation

Implementation should verify:

- Process alignment
- Documentation updates
- Training requirements
- Supporting standards
- Operational readiness
- Assigned responsibilities

Implementation should translate policy into operational practice.

---

# Version Management

Every policy should maintain:

- Version number
- Revision history
- Approval history
- Effective dates
- Superseded versions
- Change summary

Version control should preserve historical traceability.

---

# Policy Exception Management

Exceptions should document:

- Policy reference
- Business justification
- Risk assessment
- Compensating controls
- Approval authority
- Expiration date

Exceptions should be periodically reviewed and formally closed.

---

# Review Cadence

Policies should be reviewed:

- On a scheduled basis
- Following regulatory changes
- After major incidents
- Following strategic changes
- During governance reviews

Review frequency should reflect organizational risk and business impact.

---

# Retirement & Archival

Retired policies should document:

- Retirement rationale
- Successor policy (if applicable)
- Approval record
- Archival location
- Historical retention requirements

Retired policies should remain available for audit and historical reference.

---

# Documentation

Policy lifecycle documentation should maintain:

- Policy register
- Approval records
- Review history
- Communication records
- Exception records
- Retirement records

Documentation should remain complete, current, and audit-ready.

---

# Continuous Improvement

Policy governance should improve through:

- Audit findings
- Regulatory updates
- Incident learnings
- Stakeholder feedback
- Governance reviews
- Operational experience

Continuous improvement should strengthen policy effectiveness over time.

---

# Governance

Policy governance requires:

- Governance Council review
- Compliance review
- Risk review
- Legal review (where applicable)
- Executive approval for enterprise-wide policies

Governance should ensure every policy remains relevant, enforceable, and aligned with organizational objectives.

---

# Success Metrics

Policy lifecycle effectiveness may be evaluated through:

- Policy review completion rate
- On-time approval rate
- Policy acknowledgment rate
- Exception trend
- Version currency
- Retirement efficiency
- Audit readiness
