# Enterprise Risk Governance Standard

> "Effective governance is not the elimination of risk—it is the disciplined management of uncertainty."

---

# Purpose

This document defines the canonical Enterprise Risk Governance Standard for Avonix AI.

It establishes the governance framework, organizational responsibilities, risk taxonomy, assessment methodology, oversight processes, and continuous improvement practices required to identify, evaluate, prioritize, treat, monitor, and communicate risks across the enterprise.

---

# Philosophy

Risk governance should be:

- Business-driven
- Proactive
- Transparent
- Evidence-based
- Consistent
- Accountable
- Continuously improving

Risk should be understood, communicated, and managed rather than ignored or assumed away.

---

# Objectives

This standard should ensure:

- Enterprise-wide risk visibility
- Consistent risk assessment
- Clear ownership
- Effective mitigation planning
- Timely escalation
- Informed decision-making
- Long-term organizational resilience

---

# Scope

Applies to:

- Strategic initiatives
- Business operations
- Technology platforms
- AI systems
- Cybersecurity
- Data assets
- Third-party services
- Regulatory obligations
- Financial exposure
- Organizational reputation

---

# Risk Governance Principles

Every risk decision should emphasize:

- Accountability
- Transparency
- Proportionality
- Traceability
- Timely action
- Continuous monitoring
- Business alignment

Risk governance should support informed innovation while protecting organizational objectives.

---

# Enterprise Risk Taxonomy

Risks should be categorized into:

- Strategic risks
- Operational risks
- Technology risks
- Cybersecurity risks
- Data risks
- AI risks
- Financial risks
- Legal risks
- Compliance risks
- Vendor and supply chain risks
- Business continuity risks
- Reputational risks

The taxonomy should remain consistent across the organization.

---

# Risk Lifecycle

Every significant risk should progress through:

```text
Identify
      ↓
Assess
      ↓
Prioritize
      ↓
Treat
      ↓
Monitor
      ↓
Review
      ↓
Close or Accept
```

Each stage should have documented ownership and decision criteria.

---

# Risk Identification

Risk identification should consider:

- Business objectives
- Architecture changes
- Operational activities
- AI capabilities
- Security findings
- Vendor dependencies
- Regulatory changes
- Incident learnings

Risk discovery should be continuous rather than event-driven.

---

# Risk Assessment

Each identified risk should evaluate:

- Likelihood
- Business impact
- Operational impact
- Financial impact
- Customer impact
- Regulatory implications
- Recovery complexity

Assessments should use standardized evaluation criteria.

---

# Risk Prioritization

Risk prioritization should consider:

- Overall risk severity
- Business criticality
- Time sensitivity
- Control effectiveness
- Organizational priorities

Higher-priority risks should receive accelerated governance attention.

---

# Risk Treatment Strategies

Treatment options may include:

- Avoid
- Reduce
- Transfer
- Accept

Selected strategies should be documented, approved, and periodically reviewed.

---

# Risk Ownership

Every significant risk should identify:

- Risk Owner
- Business Owner
- Technical Owner
- Security Owner (where applicable)
- Operations Owner
- Executive Sponsor (for enterprise risks)

Ownership should remain clear throughout the risk lifecycle.

---

# Risk Appetite & Tolerance

The organization should define:

- Enterprise risk appetite
- Domain-specific tolerance levels
- Escalation thresholds
- Approval authority for risk acceptance

Risk decisions should align with business strategy and governance objectives.

---

# Enterprise Risk Register

The enterprise risk register should maintain:

- Risk identifier
- Description
- Category
- Owner
- Current status
- Treatment strategy
- Review schedule
- Approval history

The register should serve as the authoritative source for enterprise risk tracking.

---

# Risk Monitoring & Reporting

Risk reporting should summarize:

- Open risks
- Emerging risks
- High-priority risks
- Treatment progress
- Residual risk
- Trend analysis

Reports should support executive and operational decision-making.

---

# Escalation Framework

Risks should be escalated based on:

- Severity
- Regulatory impact
- Customer impact
- Business disruption
- Executive attention required

Escalation criteria should be predefined and consistently applied.

---

# Documentation

Every governed risk should document:

- Description
- Assessment
- Ownership
- Treatment plan
- Review history
- Acceptance decisions
- Supporting evidence

Documentation should remain complete and audit-ready.

---

# Continuous Improvement

Risk governance should improve through:

- Incident reviews
- Audit findings
- Risk trend analysis
- Lessons learned
- Governance assessments
- Industry developments

Continuous improvement should strengthen enterprise resilience.

---

# Governance

Risk governance requires:

- Enterprise Risk Council review
- Architecture review
- Security review
- Compliance review
- Executive approval for high-impact risk acceptance

Governance should ensure risk decisions are transparent, justified, and aligned with organizational objectives.

---

# Success Metrics

Risk governance effectiveness may be evaluated through:

- Risk assessment completion rate
- High-risk mitigation progress
- Residual risk trend
- Escalation responsiveness
- Risk review completion
- Audit findings related to unmanaged risk
- Enterprise resilience indicators
