# Enterprise AI Governance Standard

> "Responsible AI is achieved through governance, accountability, transparency, and continuous oversight."

---

# Purpose

This document defines the canonical Enterprise AI Governance Standard for Avonix AI.

It establishes the governance framework, lifecycle management, ownership model, responsible AI principles, operational oversight, and risk management practices required to ensure that every AI capability is trustworthy, secure, explainable, compliant, and aligned with business objectives.

---

# Philosophy

AI governance should be:

- Human-centered
- Responsible
- Transparent
- Accountable
- Secure
- Risk-aware
- Continuously improving

AI should augment human decision-making while remaining subject to appropriate oversight.

---

# Objectives

This standard should ensure:

- Responsible AI adoption
- Controlled AI lifecycle management
- Trustworthy AI outcomes
- Consistent governance
- Regulatory readiness
- Sustainable AI operations
- Continuous monitoring and improvement

---

# Scope

Applies to:

- Large Language Models (LLMs)
- AI Agents
- Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
- Prompt libraries
- Knowledge bases
- Embedding services
- Vector databases
- AI workflows
- Model orchestration
- AI-assisted business processes

---

# AI Governance Principles

Every AI capability should emphasize:

- Accountability
- Transparency
- Fairness
- Reliability
- Security
- Privacy
- Human oversight
- Traceability

Governance should apply equally to internally developed and externally provided AI services.

---

# AI Lifecycle Governance

Every AI capability should progress through:

```text
Ideation
      ↓
Assessment
      ↓
Design
      ↓
Development
      ↓
Validation
      ↓
Approval
      ↓
Deployment
      ↓
Monitoring
      ↓
Improvement
      ↓
Retirement
```

Each lifecycle stage should have documented entry, exit, and approval criteria.

---

# AI Ownership Model

Each AI capability should identify:

- Business Owner
- AI Product Owner
- AI Model Steward
- Data Steward
- Engineering Owner
- Security Owner
- Risk Owner
- Operations Owner

Ownership should remain clearly documented throughout the AI lifecycle.

---

# Responsible AI Principles

Responsible AI should demonstrate:

- Fairness
- Explainability
- Transparency
- Accountability
- Privacy protection
- Human oversight
- Safety
- Reliability

These principles should guide design, deployment, and operational decisions.

---

# Model Governance

Every governed model should document:

- Business purpose
- Intended use
- Supported use cases
- Known limitations
- Version history
- Approval status
- Lifecycle stage

Models should remain traceable throughout their operational lifetime.

---

# Prompt Governance

Prompt assets should define:

- Purpose
- Owner
- Version
- Supported models
- Usage constraints
- Review history
- Retirement status

Prompt changes should follow controlled review and version management processes.

---

# RAG & Knowledge Governance

Knowledge sources should document:

- Source authority
- Ownership
- Refresh frequency
- Classification
- Retention policy
- Validation process
- Citation expectations

Knowledge quality should be periodically assessed to maintain trustworthy AI responses.

---

# AI Agent Governance

Every AI agent should define:

- Business objective
- Allowed responsibilities
- Operational boundaries
- Tool permissions
- Human escalation rules
- Failure handling
- Monitoring requirements

Agents should operate only within explicitly approved capabilities.

---

# Model Risk Management

AI risk assessments should evaluate:

- Incorrect outputs
- Hallucination risk
- Bias risk
- Privacy exposure
- Security threats
- Operational impact
- Business consequences

Risk assessments should be reviewed before production approval.

---

# Monitoring & Drift Governance

Operational monitoring should evaluate:

- Model performance
- Response quality
- Drift indicators
- Prompt effectiveness
- Knowledge freshness
- Failure rates
- User feedback trends

Significant degradation should trigger investigation and corrective action.

---

# Human Oversight

Human oversight should define:

- Review responsibilities
- Escalation criteria
- Override procedures
- Manual intervention points
- Decision accountability

Human oversight should remain proportionate to business risk.

---

# AI Auditability

AI systems should maintain records of:

- Model versions
- Prompt versions
- Knowledge source versions
- Approval history
- Significant operational events
- Governance reviews

Audit records should support operational transparency and regulatory readiness.

---

# Exception Management

AI governance exceptions should document:

- Business justification
- Risk assessment
- Compensating controls
- Expiration date
- Review schedule
- Approval authority

Exceptions should be temporary and subject to periodic review.

---

# Documentation

Every governed AI capability should document:

- Purpose
- Owner
- Lifecycle stage
- Risk classification
- Dependencies
- Monitoring approach
- Review history

Documentation should remain synchronized with operational reality.

---

# Continuous Improvement

AI governance should improve through:

- Model evaluations
- Operational reviews
- Incident learnings
- User feedback
- Governance audits
- Technology advancements

Continuous improvement should strengthen trust, quality, and operational resilience.

---

# Governance

AI governance requires:

- AI Governance Council review
- Architecture review
- Data Governance review
- Security review
- Compliance review
- Executive approval for high-impact AI capabilities

Governance should balance innovation with accountability and responsible use.

---

# Success Metrics

AI governance effectiveness may be evaluated through:

- AI approval compliance
- Governance review completion
- Model performance stability
- Drift detection responsiveness
- Knowledge freshness
- AI-related incident rate
- Responsible AI assessment results
