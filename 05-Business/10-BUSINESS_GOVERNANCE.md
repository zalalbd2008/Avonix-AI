# Business Governance

> "Strong businesses are governed by principles, not personalities. Governance creates clarity, accountability, and long-term resilience."

---

# Purpose

This document defines the canonical Business Governance architecture for Avonix AI.

It establishes:

- Governance philosophy
- Governance structure
- Decision governance
- Business performance governance
- Policy governance
- Risk oversight
- Strategic portfolio governance
- Governance metrics
- Continuous improvement

This document is the constitutional reference for every business decision.

---

# Governance Philosophy

Business Governance should ensure:

- Accountability
- Transparency
- Responsible stewardship
- Ethical leadership
- Long-term sustainability
- Customer trust

Governance should enable informed decision-making while preserving organizational agility.

---

# Governance Principles

Every governance process should be:

- Transparent
- Documented
- Repeatable
- Risk-aware
- Evidence-based
- Auditable
- Consistent

---

# Governance Objectives

The governance framework should:

- Align execution with strategy
- Clarify ownership
- Improve decision quality
- Reduce organizational risk
- Protect stakeholders
- Support sustainable growth

---

# Governance Structure

Business Governance consists of:

## Executive Leadership

Responsible for:

- Vision
- Strategy
- Investment
- Organizational priorities

---

## Functional Leadership

Responsible for:

- Department execution
- Budget ownership
- KPI achievement
- Operational performance

---

## Governance Committees

Committees may oversee:

- Product Strategy
- AI Governance
- Security
- Risk
- Finance
- Compliance
- Operations

Each committee should have a documented charter.

---

# Decision Governance

Every significant decision should define:

- Decision owner
- Stakeholders
- Required evidence
- Risks
- Expected outcomes
- Approval authority

Major decisions should be documented using Architecture Decision Records (ADRs) where applicable.

---

# Decision Hierarchy

Business decisions may be categorized as:

- Strategic
- Tactical
- Operational
- Emergency

Each category should have a defined approval path.

---

# Delegation of Authority

Authority should be delegated according to:

- Financial thresholds
- Business impact
- Risk level
- Regulatory implications

Delegation should never obscure accountability.

---

# RACI Framework

Critical business initiatives should define:

- Responsible
- Accountable
- Consulted
- Informed

Role clarity reduces execution risk.

---

# Escalation Framework

Escalations should define:

- Trigger conditions
- Escalation owner
- Response expectations
- Resolution authority
- Documentation requirements

Escalation paths should remain simple and well understood.

---

# Business Performance Governance

Executive governance should review:

- OKRs
- KPIs
- Revenue
- Customer Success
- Operational efficiency
- Financial health
- Strategic initiatives

Performance reviews should focus on trends and business outcomes.

---

# Policy Governance

Every policy should include:

- Purpose
- Scope
- Owner
- Version
- Approval history
- Review frequency
- Related documents

Policies should remain version-controlled.

---

# Strategic Portfolio Governance

The organization should govern:

- Product investments
- Platform initiatives
- AI initiatives
- Market expansion
- Operational improvements
- Strategic partnerships

Portfolio decisions should balance value, risk, and capacity.

---

# Risk Governance

Business Governance should oversee:

- Enterprise risks
- Financial risks
- Operational risks
- Legal risks
- Cybersecurity risks
- AI risks
- Reputational risks

Every significant risk should have:

- Owner
- Severity
- Mitigation plan
- Review cadence

---

# Financial Governance

Financial oversight should include:

- Budget approval
- Investment reviews
- Forecast validation
- Cost management
- Profitability monitoring

Financial governance should support sustainable growth.

---

# Compliance Oversight

Governance should verify:

- Policy compliance
- Regulatory readiness
- Audit completion
- Control effectiveness
- Corrective actions

Compliance reporting should be timely and evidence-based.

---

# Strategic Planning Cadence

Governance activities should occur on a defined cadence.

## Annual

- Corporate strategy
- Budget approval
- Investment planning

---

## Quarterly

- OKR review
- Portfolio review
- Risk assessment

---

## Monthly

- KPI review
- Financial review
- Operational review

---

## Weekly

- Executive priorities
- Escalations
- Cross-functional coordination

---

# Governance Metrics

Governance effectiveness should monitor:

- Decision turnaround time
- OKR completion
- Policy review completion
- Audit findings
- Risk closure rate
- Executive action completion
- Strategic initiative success
- Governance maturity

Metrics should improve governance—not create bureaucracy.

---

# Documentation Standards

Governance artifacts should include:

- Policies
- ADRs
- Meeting decisions
- Executive reports
- Audit reports
- Risk registers
- Board materials

Documentation should remain centralized and version-controlled.

---

# Continuous Improvement

The governance framework should evolve through:

- Annual governance reviews
- Internal audits
- Lessons learned
- Customer feedback
- Employee feedback
- Industry best practices

Governance maturity should improve over time.

---

# Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- Undefined ownership
- Informal decision-making
- Policy duplication
- Governance without accountability
- Excessive bureaucracy
- Decisions without documented rationale

Good governance should accelerate good decisions—not delay them.

---

# Governance Review Checklist

Every governance initiative should answer:

- Is ownership clearly defined?
- Is decision authority documented?
- Are policies current?
- Are KPIs measurable?
- Are risks reviewed?
- Is compliance verified?
- Is documentation complete?
- Are improvement actions assigned?
